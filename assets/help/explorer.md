# Explorer

The Explorer is a knowledge web over the app's bundled study datasets **and your own study data**: every **person**, **place**, **event**, **topic**, **passage**, and **tag** gets its own page, and the pages all link to each other. Start anywhere and follow the connections—from a person to the events in their life, from an event to where it happened, from a place to everyone mentioned there, from a chapter to its topics. It works completely offline.

Open it from the hamburger menu (App Drawer) → **Explorer**.

## Searching
Type at least two letters into the search box to search everything at once—people (including alternate names), places, events, topics, passages, and your tags. You can also type a reference like `John 1` to open that chapter's page directly, or a tag name (with or without the `#`) to find your tags. Tap any result to open its page.

Before you type anything, the Explorer's home screen also offers **Feasts** and **Bible Stories** rows—scrollable shortcuts into the same curated topics, for when you'd rather browse than search.

## Entity Pages
Each page is a set of cards showing everything the datasets know, and every entry on a card is a link to another page:

- **People** — an Easton's biography, family members (tap a name to open their page), the events they took part in, a map of the places in their story, every verse where they appear, and a **Their stories** card linking to any curated Bible story or feast their verses appear in (so David's page links to "David and Goliath"). An icon opens the full-screen **Family Tree**—see the **Study Tools** help topic (under People → Family Tree) for how it works.
- **Places** — an interactive map, the events that happened there, the people mentioned there, dictionary definitions, and the verses that mention it.
- **Events** — when it happened, the scripture account, who was there, and a map of where it took place.
- **Topics** — the topic's entries from Nave's Topical Bible with their passages, plus dictionary definitions. Hand-curated feasts and well-known Bible stories are topics too, and their pages additionally aggregate places, commentaries, cross-references, your notes, tags, and media across every chapter the story touches.
- **Passages** — the people, places (with a map), events, and topics found in a chapter, alongside every installed commentary's entries for it, **your own notes** anchored there, and **your tags** on its verses.

Tap any verse chip to jump straight to that passage in the Reader. Any map you find on a page has an expand icon that opens it as the full-screen, interactive **Atlas**—see the **Study Tools** help topic (under People → Atlas) for what it can do, including tracing a person's journey.

## Your Tags in the Web
Tags (see **Notes, Highlights & Sharing**) weave your own study into the Explorer:

- **Tag pages** — open a tag to see everything filed under it: tagged verses with their text, plus your notes, sermons, journals, and prayers. Tap a verse to read it, or tap any other item to open it right where it lives (the sermon editor, the journal editor, the Prayers tab). The page also cross-references what's *in* those tagged verses—**People**, **Places** (with a map you can expand into the full-screen Atlas), and **Events** cards drawn from the tagged passages—plus a **Media** card for any of your own photo/PDF attachments (see **Study Tools**) that you've filed directly under this tag. An **Explore their chapters** card links each tagged verse's chapter back into the knowledge web, and a **Related tags** card surfaces tags that share items with this one.
- **Your tags cards** — person, place, and event pages show the tags you've put on verses where that entity appears; passage pages show the tags in that chapter. So Elijah's page can remind you that you tagged his showdown at Carmel `#prayer`—and one tap later you're looking at everything else you filed there.

The Explorer never edits your tags—rename, recolor, or delete them from the **Tags** view.

## Breadcrumb Trail
As you drill from page to page, a breadcrumb bar tracks your path. Tap any crumb to jump back to that point, use the back arrow to step back once, or tap the home icon (or the search icon in the app bar) to start a new search. The trail is remembered while the app is open, so reopening the Explorer resumes right where you left off.

> 💡 **Tip:** For focused, chapter-tied views of the same data while you read, see the **Topics**, **Places**, and **People** tools in the Study Tools panel. The Explorer is the free-roaming version—best when one question leads to another.
